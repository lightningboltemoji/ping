# The notch

> Change `1787212322` — 2026-08-20

## Goal

Ping had two effects and a gap between them. The glow is area and no identity — it says *something*
is badged. The shelf is detail — icons, names, counts — and it costs a piece of screen you were
using. Neither answers "who?" quietly. The notch is the third point: **identity without detail**, in
the one strip of screen that is already dead space and where nothing of yours lives. A black strip
hangs from the top edge, the width of the notch plus an extension either side, holding one indicator
per badged app; on a MacBook it grows out of the real cutout, and on a screen without one it draws
the notch too. It rests on the spike in `Spikes/Notch/`, which settled that every number it needs is
public API and that a window of ours can live in the menu bar strip at all.

## Implementation

**The config surface is small on purpose.** `[notch]` describes the strip; an app writes
`notch = { … }` to subscribe and takes only `enabled` and `color`. `style` is deliberately not
per-app: the notch is one shared object, and indicators disagreeing about their own shape is the same
mistake as a per-app shelf anchor. There is no `color` at the `[notch]` level either — colour is the
only thing telling one indicator from another, so a global default would make every dot the same one;
the built-in is red, the Dock badge's own, which is what makes `notch = {}` already legible.
Resolution is one layer rather than the glow's four-layer cascade, and returns the same three `nil`s.

**Two things sit a level lower than the plan sketched them.** The `count`-falls-back-to-`dot` rule
(there are no digits to draw for a bare dot) is in `Config.resolvedNotch(for:badge:)` rather than in
the renderer, so it is pinned by `ResolutionTests` with the rest of the truth table — which means
`NotchItem` carries a resolved `style` and the renderer keeps deciding nothing. `NotchLayout` returns
a width per side rather than one `extensionWidth`, because `placement = "left"` must not widen the
side carrying nothing; `split` is where the two are equal, and equalising them is what keeps an odd
number of indicators from tipping the notch.

**The animation is one layer, and the asymmetry is what shaped it.** The shape's `bounds` and
`position` animate together, and its bounds are centred on itself — so an indicator is positioned
from the notch rather than from an edge that is moving, and the opening strip *reveals* indicators
that never move. Rejected: three layers (a static core plus two extensions), which puts rounded
corners at the seams where they cannot be; and animating `bounds.size.width` alone, as the spike did,
which anchors children to the left edge — fine for one dot, but it makes the right extension's
indicators slide inward while the left's ride outward.

**Absent means retracted, not gone.** `RenderState.notch` is `nil` when the strip should not be on
screen, and the renderer closes to the cutout it grew out of (or to nothing, on a stand-in) rather
than dropping the window: the common case for `nil` is the last badge clearing, and an effect that
vanished mid-motion would arrive one way and leave another. `always-show` is the other side of that
line — it produces a state that is *present with no items*, which is what separates "nothing badged"
from "the effect is off".

**The tail takes turns rather than being dropped**, per `PRINCIPLES.md` §3, so the strip cannot
under-report the Dock. `GlowRotator` became `Rotator<Element>` instead of growing a second LRU beside
it. It needs a clock of its own, because a badge that does not change produces no render to ride on;
that clock is a three-second constant in `NotchRenderer` rather than an eleventh key, and if living
with it says otherwise, it is `[notch]`'s to add. A `max-width` too small for even one slot shows no
indicator at all — the budget is the user's word, and the shape still says *something*.

Smaller calls: `NotchSettings.enabled` is a plain `Bool` like the shelf's rather than `GlowSettings`'
optional-and-`isEnabled`, because nothing inherits it; the schema carries ten entries, not the plan's
eight, which predates the last two keys; a preview whose scope is the notch *alone* always gets the
strip, so the Notch tab can never preview an empty screen; and `IconCache` came out of `ShelfRenderer`
now that two renderers want icons.

**Verified by running the binary** against `BADGES` on a 14" MacBook Pro (frame 1800×1169, cutout
220×38 at x=790): the extension grows out of the real notch with no visible seam, `split` puts the
first half left and the rest right with both sides at the wider one's width, the `count` style draws
Chango digits in each app's colour, the overflow slot changes hands, and clearing the badges retracts
the strip to exactly the cutout. The two spike scripts were reformatted, because `make lint` is a
gate and they were failing it.

## Observations

- **The `icon` style is not verified visually.** It needs an `appURL`, which only a real Dock reading
  carries, and the debug binary has no Accessibility grant; without one it falls back to a dot, which
  is the path that was exercised.
- **The stand-in notch is not verified on hardware** — the only screen here has a real one. The code
  path differs in two places: the strip's height comes from the menu bar rather than the safe area,
  and the shape grows from zero width instead of from the cutout.
- **The settings window was not driven interactively.** The Notch tab is the schema walked, so it has
  no code of its own; the Apps tab's new rows and the preview scoping are covered by tests only.
- **A `count` indicator spills into the gaps beside its slot.** Slots are uniform — `item-size` wide —
  and digits are not, which is fine at two digits and tightens at three.
- Three questions the spike could not answer are still open: whether an extension that covers other
  apps' status items is acceptable in use (which is what `max-width`'s default should be tuned by),
  whether the notch wants motion between arrival and retraction (if so, a `[notch.pulse]` of its own
  rather than the glow's), and whether `width` should default to 185 — exact on a 14" panel, and
  arbitrary on a 27" display.
