# A mirrored notch

> Change `1787630669` — 2026-08-24

## Goal

`placement` could put the indicators left of the cutout, right of it, or halve the list across both.
All three can leave the strip lopsided: `left` and `right` open one side only, and `split` matches
the two widths but not their contents. There was no way to ask for a notch that is symmetric in what
it draws as well as in how far it opens.

## Implementation

`mirror` is a fourth `NotchPlacement`: the whole list on each side, the right one reversed, so the
two extensions are reflections of each other about the cutout's middle. Slots are numbered left to
right, which makes a reversed array a reflected side and costs the renderer nothing. Reflected
rather than translated — `p0 p1 p2 [ ] p2 p1 p0` rather than a second copy reading the same way —
because the reflection is what gives the strip an axis of symmetry, and it makes both extensions
read outward-in: the newest indicator lands against the cutout on both sides and the older ones
slide outward together. A translated copy would have the two sides growing in opposite directions.

Capacity is the difference between `mirror` and `split`: `split` spends the two budgets on one list
and so holds twice a side's capacity, `mirror` spends them on two copies and holds one side's.

The tail forced the one structural change. `overflowSide: NotchSide?` could not say "both", and
appending its slot to each side would have put it against the cutout on the left and at the outer
edge on the right — the one asymmetry a mirrored strip cannot have. It becomes
`overflowSlots: [NotchSide: Int]`, which says *which* slot as well as which side, and the tail's
slot on a reflected right extension is 0, the one against the cutout. Rejected: keeping the optional
side and having `NotchStrip` duplicate the tail when the placement is `mirror`, which puts a
placement test in the renderer, and the layout would no longer describe what is on screen.

That index is not something the renderer should be reading, so the layout now hands it slot contents
directly: `contents(on:)` returns `[NotchItem?]` in slot order with `nil` for the tail's slot. The
strip enumerates it and substitutes whichever app is taking its turn, which deletes the special case
that placed the tail separately.

**Verified by running the binary** on the 14" MacBook Pro's own cutout (220×38 at x=790, the screen
at 1800×1169), `item-size` 10 and `gap` 8, so a side pays for six slots. Four badged apps in
distinct colours drew `● ● ● ● [cutout] ● ● ● ●` with the colour order reversed across it and the
two extensions equal. Eight badged apps drew five colours plus the shared slot on each side, that
slot against the cutout on both, holding the same app at the same moment and changing hands together
on `NotchRenderer`'s three-second clock.

## Observations

- The stand-in notch was not looked at. It enters the arithmetic only as `geometry.rect.width`, and
  a strip growing from a zero-width resting shape is symmetric under `mirror` for the same reason a
  real one is.
- `mirror` draws each indicator twice, so a strip at capacity holds twice the layers of a `split`
  one showing the same apps. Nothing here is per-frame — the indicators do not animate — so this is
  a layer count, not work per tick.
- `style = "count"` under `mirror` reflects the slots, not the glyphs — a count reads the right way
  round on both sides rather than backwards, which is the useful answer and not a literal
  reflection. Digits are centred in their slot, so a wide one still spills evenly and the two sides
  balance.
